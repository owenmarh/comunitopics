As we may think

Bush's piece was a pretty difficult read for me. Very science heavy and focused primarily on logic not form. One passage that caught my attention was: "We know that when the eye sees, all the consequent information is transmitted to the brain by means of electrical vibrations in the channel of the optic nerve. This is an exact analogy with the electrical vibrations which occur in the cable of a television set: they convey the picture from the photocells which see it to the radio transmitter from which it is broadcast. We know further that if we can approach that cable with the proper instruments, we do not need to touch it; we can pick up those vibrations by electrical induction and thus discover and reproduce the scene which is being transmitted, just as a telephone wire may be tapped for its message." To describe the science of transmitting images before the tech existed is wild. Bush's writing is full of these moments, and I admittedly cannot catalogue even half of them because his descriptions of each are so dense. An interesting element of the passage above is Bush's analogy of the visual "information transmitted to the brain as electrical vibrations in a channel [to the] electrical vibrations which occur in the cable of a television set," with both transmitting a picture to Bush's 'photocells' which transfer it to the radio transmitter itself. Going more in depth on this passage reveals my initial understanding of it was wrong. I've been reading this whole essay as a list of predictions for the future that (because we're reading this in 2026) I assumed inevitably came true. I've been jumping to conclusions before they arrive. This ended up being more of a reflection of my reading experience than of the reading itself. Interesting to know the author's received a response - probably many, to this reading - that his messages have not clearly transmitted. The piece is so mired in pure and logical complex statements that its nature is in complete opposition to common vernacular.
