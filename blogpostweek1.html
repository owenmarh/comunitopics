At the conclusion of Hito Steyerl's 'In Defense of the Poor Image,' she declares the poor image's present-day condition to be "no longer about the real thing -- the originary original." Rather, she continues, the poor image is defined by -- or in Steyerl's words, 'about' -- the conditions that enable its continued circulation. Steyerl's concluding words contain an implied chronology. She traces the evolution of the poor image from a copy of the high-grade original to an object whose form is an aesthetic product of its circulation, and whose initial meaning as a copy has been overlaid by its present and continued becoming of meaning as re-processed debris. The poor image's degraded form betrays the conditions of its circulation -- reprocessing, exchanging, archiving, and the like. Through this process, the poor image's initial condition as a copy is eclipsed by its significance as an object of accessibility. But I also do not mean to impose a hierarchy of meaning, as during its degradation, the poor image most times retains its legibility as a copy. One might be able to apply this evolutionary theory to works of obscure cinema, whose after-lives depend on their continued circulation. But this is only one example of how a poor image emerges. What may be more instructive for our class is Steyerl's comparison of poor images to an imperfect cinema. Steyerl explains imperfect cinema as "one that strives to overcome the divisions of labor in a class society," and further, that which "merges art with life and science, blurring the distinction between consumer and producer." Blurring the distinction between consumer and producer. The consumer of the poor image is also (in part) the producer of the very conditions which create it. Now let's turn to the text-based game, our current assignment. My intention behind this assignment is precisely this collapse. What, then does it mean to create a work whose original intention is to create poor images themselves (complete with their associated conditions)? The interactive text-based game by its very nature is suited for this challenge, given its meaning is completed -- and continued to be created -- through the player's actions. The (initial) creator of the game builds a world through a set of rules and conditions (i.e. X leading to Y, Z leading to A). Yet, the (initial) creator does not choose how people play the game. There is a set of possibilities, whose paths emerge chronologically in the choices of the player. Thus, the text-based game's meaning as a poor image is practically built into its structure, its set of conditions allowing a repeated re-scrambling and re-experiencing of the work itself.
