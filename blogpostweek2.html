[TW: Discussion of genocide and mass violence] I read Walter Benjamin's Art in the Age of Mechanical Reproduction in my first semester of undergrad for an introductory seminar class. Re-visiting the text now reveals that my first interpretation only scraped the surface of its meaning. More specifically, while my encounter with the essay at 18 might have been my inauguration into the art theory milieu, reading the text again in my mid-20s reveals an obvious Marxian undercurrent, whose complexities I'm just beginning to understand. In the Epilogue of the text, Benjamin asserts that while fascism aestheticizes politics, communism politicizes aesthetics. Similarly, fascism gives the masses an expression - albeit, not a self-directed one - while leaving property relations untouched. In contrast, communism (as the inverse, politicizing aesthetics and all) seeks to disrupt property relations by freeing the masses to express themselves. It is the difference between these two opposite poles that I understand now undergird Benjamin's argument. Just as interesting was Benjamin's prediction of the new art's (film, or the reproduced image) psychological effect on mobilizing the masses: "The distracted person...can form habits. More, the ability to master certain tasks in a state of distraction proves that their solution has become a matter of habit. Distraction as provided by art presents a covert control of the extent to which new tasks have become soluble by apperception. Since, moreover, individuals are tempted to avoid such tasks, art will tackle the most difficult and most important ones where it is able to mobilize the masses" (19). Benjamin's prediction may have come true somewhat during, for instance, the Arab Spring, whose social upheaval was aided in large part by social media as a way to organize demonstrations and disseminate information. This example in part resembles Benjamin's hopes. Yet atrocities such as the Gaza genocide, whose horrific nature is sometimes transmitted to pro-Palestine social media users via Palestinians in Gaza appealing for help on IG reels, seem to often subordinate social media users to a stasis, confronted with the gravity of atrocities whose scale is too great for them or even an organized movement to defeat (speaking only as a resident of the U.S). Even when such reels are accompanied by material goals -- whose significance cannot be understated -- the systemic killing machine remains intact. I apologize for the heavy subject, but I think it's important for us to understand (speaking for myself at least), how such images, moving or otherwise, might spur a mass mobilization strong enough to counteract the power of global, genocidal imperialism. How might art free the masses to express themselves and in turn reorganize the very property relations and institutions whose consolidation and violence fascism seeks to uphold through an entirely opposite, reductive and immediate media strategy?
