The first two chapters of Critical Art Ensemble's The Electronic Disturbance have a multitude of threads to pull from that I won't be able to wholly capture in this blog post, so here are a couple that struck me.

Arguing the modern form of elite power as evolved from its previously sedentary state, CAE notes that, "having rid itself of its national and urban bases to wander in absence on the electronic pathways, [elite power] can no longer be disrupted by strategies predicated upon the contestation of sedentary forces" (23). Meanwhile, the authors note, "The architectural monuments of power are hollow and empty, and function now only as bunkers for the complicit and those who acquiesce...secure places...[that] silence resistance and resentment by the signs of resolution, continuity, commodification, and nostalgia" (23).

CAE then makes the analogy of the web to that of the street as the connective tissue between and around the bunkers. In these marginal spaces whose public function is, in a way, subordinated through the privileging of the bunker as a safe and thus legitimate private space, those who occupy the street are constructed as liabilities to the safety of those who seek security in the bunkers. Yet, "the price of this protectionism" -- that is, the safety bunker occupants are fed -- "is the relinquishment of individual sovereignty...[acting] as a seductive agent offering the credible illusion of consumptive choice and ideological peace for the complicit or...an aggressive force demanding acquiescence for the resistance [to it/elite power]" (28).

There's a lot to say about the concept of the bunker and the streets in-between. The bunkers seem to be constructed to appease the general populace as long as they continue to play the role of the consumer. And as long as they continue to see those who cannot consume (those without money, the marginalized) as a danger to their freedom (to consume). This analysis applies to both the virtual and spatial realm -- a consistent application throughout CAE's text. More to say about that in the discussion next week!
